A financial intelligence note was prepared following analysis of the transaction histories obtained from the mule accounts recovered during the Mundka raid. Analysts traced consolidated transfers from at least eleven of the mule accounts to a single intermediate account before the funds were withdrawn in cash across three branches over a two-day window. Bank branch CCTV and account-opening records identified the person collecting the cash withdrawals as an authorised representative acting on behalf of an entity named in the account-opening form as N. Contractor Forex & Remittance, with a registered contact number that investigators subsequently linked to Naseem Contractor.

The note records that approximately four crore sixty lakh rupees passed through this channel over the review period, consistent with the losses reported by complainants in the associated FIR, and that the pattern of same-day cash withdrawal following consolidation is typical of hawala settlement rather than ordinary banking activity. Investigators flagged that the name Naseem Contractor, along with the initials "N.E.C." appearing on an earlier transaction slip recovered from the same intermediate account, corresponds to an individual independently referenced in a separate narcotics-related structuring inquiry currently under investigation by a district unit in Punjab, though no formal connection between the two matters has yet been established beyond the coincidence of identity. The note recommends that the two investigating units be put in contact to determine whether Contractor's exchange operation is functioning as a shared laundering channel across unrelated criminal networks, and that his known bank accounts and exchange premises be placed under continued financial surveillance pending further orders.
