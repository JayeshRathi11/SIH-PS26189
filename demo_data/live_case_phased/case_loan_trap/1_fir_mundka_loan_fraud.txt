An FIR was registered at Mundka Police Station, Delhi, after a complainant reported that he had been contacted by a caller claiming to represent a nationalised bank and offering a pre-approved personal loan against payment of a processing fee. The complainant stated that after transferring the fee to an account number provided over the call, he was asked for further "insurance" and "GST" payments totalling approximately one lakh eighty thousand rupees before he realised no loan disbursement was forthcoming and the number became unreachable. A review of similar complaints received by the cyber cell over the preceding month identified a recurring pattern involving at least thirty other complainants across four states, with total reported losses assessed at approximately four crore eighty lakh rupees.

Technical surveillance traced the calls to a rented office in the Mundka industrial area operating under the name of a purported financial consultancy. A raid on the premises led to the detention of seven persons operating call stations, along with a supervisor identified as Devraj Oberoi, known among the staff as "DJ," who is believed to coordinate script distribution and daily collection targets for the operation. Recovered material included several mobile phones, a set of bank passbooks and debit cards issued in the names of individuals not present at the premises, and a notebook listing account numbers against code names. A separate module operating from an adjoining room was found to be running a parallel extortion racket in which victims were contacted after having shared personal images through a fraudulent video-call application, and threatened with circulation of the material unless payment was made; this module was found to share staff, premises and at least two bank accounts with the loan-fraud operation.

Preliminary enquiry identified Priyanka Solanki, arrested at the premises, as the person responsible for procuring what staff referred to as "kits" — sets of bank account credentials, SIM cards and passbooks obtained from individuals paid a small commission to open accounts in their own names. Investigators believe the funds collected through both modules were consolidated before being moved out of the mule accounts, though the onward destination of the funds had not been established at the time of this filing. The FIR was registered at approximately 16:20 hrs and the matter has been marked for continued financial investigation, with a request made to the banks concerned for complete transaction histories of the accounts recovered from the premises.
