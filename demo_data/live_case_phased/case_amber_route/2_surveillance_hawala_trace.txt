Following the Ajnala Road seizure, a surveillance team was deployed to observe the residence and known associates of Iqbal Deol over a three-day period. On the second day, Deol was observed leaving a relative's house in a grey Honda City and travelling to a currency exchange office on the outskirts of Amritsar city, where he remained for approximately twenty minutes. Records obtained from the exchange office under authorisation identified the proprietor as Naseem Contractor, who operates the exchange as a front for an informal hawala counter and is known to district police from an earlier, unrelated inquiry into unlicensed money transfer. A ledger recovered from the premises, written largely in code, contained entries against the initials "N.E.C." that investigators believe correspond to Contractor himself, alongside a series of numeric entries that field officers assess to be settlement instructions rather than ordinary currency transactions.

A subsequent call intercept, obtained under appropriate authorisation, recorded a conversation between Deol and a number registered to Contractor, in which Deol is heard confirming that "the Ajnala lot did not clear" and instructing that "the Dubai side" be informed that the next settlement would be delayed. Contractor is heard acknowledging and stating that he would "adjust it against the Delhi account for now." Investigators note that this reference to a separate "Delhi account" is consistent with intelligence, unconnected to the present case, that has independently flagged an individual using the initials N.E.C. in relation to a Delhi-based fund-transfer inquiry currently being examined by a different investigating unit. No further individuals were identified during this surveillance window. The matter has been forwarded for continued monitoring, with a recommendation that Contractor's exchange office be placed under sustained observation and that his known contacts outside Punjab be traced.
